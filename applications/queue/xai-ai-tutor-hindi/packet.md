1. **Is Hindi genuinely your first/native language?** The JD requires "native Hindi proficiency."
   Your resume lists Hindi, Bengali and English — if Bengali is actually your mother tongue and
   Hindi is a strong second language, say so honestly in the application; don't claim "native"
   if it isn't accurate. Native-level fluency is a hard requirement they will test.

   getting feedback on them. Make sure that's something you're genuinely fine with.
3. They usually run a **language/audio assessment or sample task** as part of the application —
   expect that, it's normal for this role, not a red flag.

## Honest match report
**Real match:** bilingual Hindi/English, clear phone-voice communication history, strong
documentation/accuracy habits, comfortable with ambiguous/noisy source material, quick to learn
new software, reliable remote setup.
**Gap — be upfront, don't oversell:** no linguistics/phonetics background, no prior audio
annotation, transcription, or voice-acting experience. That's fine — it's an entry point role for
many people — but don't claim tooling experience you don't have. The resume is written to lead
with genuinely transferable strengths (communication, accuracy, tool-adoption speed) rather than
inventing audio-industry experience.
